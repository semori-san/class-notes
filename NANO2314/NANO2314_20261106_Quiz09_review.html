Quiz 9 — November 6, 2026

  1. 1. (2 pts) A town of 1000 people is split into three groups: Healthy, Sick, and Recovering. Every week, people move between the groups at the rates shown in the diagram. For example, the arrow from Healthy to Sick labeled 10% means that every week 10% of the healthy people become sick. The total population stays the same.

    Let hnh_{n}, sns_{n}, and rnr_{n} be the numbers of healthy, sick, and recovering people in week nn. Write a system of three equations that gives hnh_{n}, sns_{n}, and rnr_{n} in terms of the numbers in the week prior, hn−1h_{n-1}, sn−1s_{n-1}, and rn−1r_{n-1}.

    Solution for question 1

    each group this week = the people who stay + the people who arrive, both from last week

    ↓ Healthy: 90% stay; arrivals from Sick and Recovering

    hn=0.90hn−1+0.15sn−1+0.40rn−1h_{n} = 0.90\,h_{n-1} + 0.15\,s_{n-1} + 0.40\,r_{n-1}

    ↓ the same for Sick and Recovering

    sn=0.10hn−1+0.55sn−1+0.20rn−1s_{n} = 0.10\,h_{n-1} + 0.55\,s_{n-1} + 0.20\,r_{n-1}

    rn=0hn−1+0.30sn−1+0.40rn−1r_{n} = 0\,h_{n-1} + 0.30\,s_{n-1} + 0.40\,r_{n-1}

    Why: Sick loses 15% + 30% = 45%, so 55% stay; Recovering loses 20% + 40% = 60%, so 40% stay. No arrow goes from Healthy to Recovering, so that term is 0.

    In words. Each new number is the people who stay in the group (100% minus everyone who leaves it) plus the people who arrive from the other groups (each arrow’s rate times last week’s number in the group the arrow starts from).

  2. 2. (2 pts) Rewrite your equations as one eigenvalue equation. It should have a 3×3 matrix, the eigenvalue λ\lambda, and the eigenvector (hsr)\begin{pmatrix} h \\ s \\ r \end{pmatrix} appearing on both sides of the equation.

    Solution for question 2

    the numbers on hn−1h_{n-1}, sn−1s_{n-1}, rn−1r_{n-1} in each equation become one row of the matrix

    (hnsnrn)=(0.900.150.400.100.550.2000.300.40)(hn−1sn−1rn−1)\begin{pmatrix} h_{n} \\ s_{n} \\ r_{n} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.90 & 0.15 & 0.40 \\ 0.10 & 0.55 & 0.20 \\ 0 & 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} h_{n-1} \\ s_{n-1} \\ r_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}

    ↓ drop subscripts; left side becomes λ times the vector

    Conclusion

    (0.900.150.400.100.550.2000.300.40)(hsr)=λ(hsr)\begin{pmatrix} 0.90 & 0.15 & 0.40 \\ 0.10 & 0.55 & 0.20 \\ 0 & 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} h \\ s \\ r \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} h \\ s \\ r \end{pmatrix}

    Why: an eigenvector comes out of the matrix as a multiple of itself, so the same vector, with no subscripts, appears on both sides; λ is that multiple.

    In words. Put each equation’s three rates in a row of the matrix, in the order h, s, r. For an eigenvector, multiplying by the matrix gives back the same vector times a number λ.

  3. 3. (2 pts) How many eigenvalues does the matrix in Problem 2 have? Out of those, why is λ=1\lambda = 1 the physically reasonable one for this town?

    Solution for question 3

    How many: 3. A 3×3 matrix has three eigenvalues (here 1, 0.6 and 0.25; you were not asked to find them).

    Why λ = 1: the total population is conserved, so a population that has settled must stay the same from week to week: multiplying by the matrix gives back 1×1 \times the same vector. Any other λ would scale the whole population every week — λ < 1 would shrink it and λ > 1 would grow it. (The eigenvectors for 0.6 and 0.25 also have negative entries, which cannot be numbers of people.)

  4. 4. (2 pts) Verify that the vector (h,s,r)=(700,200,100)(h, s, r) = (700,\ 200,\ 100) is an eigenvector with λ=1\lambda = 1, by multiplying your matrix from Problem 2 by this vector. Show the multiplication, and state what you conclude.

    Solution for question 4

    each row of the matrix times (700,200,100)(700, 200, 100), entry by entry, then add across the row

    0.90(700)+0.15(200)+0.40(100)=630+30+40=7000.90(700) + 0.15(200) + 0.40(100) = 630 + 30 + 40 = 700

    0.10(700)+0.55(200)+0.20(100)=70+110+20=2000.10(700) + 0.55(200) + 0.20(100) = 70 + 110 + 20 = 200

    0(700)+0.30(200)+0.40(100)=0+60+40=1000(700) + 0.30(200) + 0.40(100) = 0 + 60 + 40 = 100

    Why: add across each row, not down each column. Adding down the columns also gives 700, 200, 100 here, but only because each column adds up to 1; it is not matrix-times-vector multiplication.

    Conclusion

    (0.900.150.400.100.550.2000.300.40)(700200100)=(700200100)=1⋅(700200100)\begin{pmatrix} 0.90 & 0.15 & 0.40 \\ 0.10 & 0.55 & 0.20 \\ 0 & 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 700 \\ 200 \\ 100 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 700 \\ 200 \\ 100 \end{pmatrix} = 1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 700 \\ 200 \\ 100 \end{pmatrix} an eigenvector with λ = 1

    In words. Row by row, the matrix turns (700, 200, 100) back into (700, 200, 100). The result is 1 times the vector, so it is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 1.

  5. 5. (2 pts) What does the eigenvector (700,200,100)(700,\ 200,\ 100) physically tell you about the town as time goes on?

    Solution for question 5

    λ = 1: one week of moving leaves (700,200,100)(700, 200, 100) unchanged

    ↓ people keep moving, but the weekly flows balance

    Conclusion

    The town settles at 700 healthy, 200 sick and 100 recovering (70%, 20% and 10%), and those numbers stay the same week after week.

    In words. After a long time, the town reaches an equilibrium of 700 healthy, 200 sick and 100 recovering people. People still move between the groups every week, but as many enter each group as leave it, so the three numbers no longer change.

  6. EC. (1 pt) Extra credit. Show that the vectors (0.6,0.8)(0.6,\ 0.8) and (−0.8,0.6)(-0.8,\ 0.6) are normalized and orthogonal.

    Solution for question EC

    length of (0.6,0.8)(0.6, 0.8): 0.62+0.82=0.36+0.64=1\sqrt{0.6^{2} + 0.8^{2}} = \sqrt{0.36 + 0.64} = 1

    length of (−0.8,0.6)(-0.8, 0.6): (−0.8)2+0.62=0.64+0.36=1\sqrt{(-0.8)^{2} + 0.6^{2}} = \sqrt{0.64 + 0.36} = 1

    ↓ both lengths are 1: normalized

    (0.6)(−0.8)+(0.8)(0.6)=−0.48+0.48=0(0.6)(-0.8) + (0.8)(0.6) = -0.48 + 0.48 = 0

    ↓ the dot product is 0: orthogonal

    In words. Normalized means length 1: the square root of the sum of the squares is 1 for both vectors. Orthogonal means the dot product is 0: multiply matching entries and add, and you get 0.

Where the class had the most trouble

These cost the most points. If you lost points here, start your review with them.

  1. Extra credit — what “normalized” and “orthogonal” mean. Only 2 of 13 booklets earned the full point. Reasons such as “they point in opposite directions”, “they have the same magnitude” or “the signs are reversed” do not show either property. Normalized means length 1: take the square root of the sum of the squares. Orthogonal means dot product 0: multiply matching entries and add.
  2. Problem 4 — multiply across the rows. Three booklets added the products down each column of the matrix instead of across each row. That happens to give 700, 200 and 100 back, but only because every column adds up to 1; it is not how a matrix multiplies a vector. Two more booklets set the rows up correctly and slipped in the arithmetic. Each row times the vector, then add.
  3. Problem 5 — say what settles, with the numbers. Three booklets said only that the total stays at 1000 (given in the problem), or named just one group, or only compared the groups. The eigenvector says the town settles at 700 healthy, 200 sick and 100 recovering, and stays there week after week.
  4. Problem 2 — the same vector on both sides. Two booklets kept subscripts on one side (hnh_n, sns_n, rnr_n) and one put λ in twice (in the matrix and in front of the vector). In an eigenvalue equation the same vector, with no subscripts, appears on both sides, and λ appears once.
  5. Problem 3 — the count. Two booklets wrote 1 or 2 eigenvalues. A 3×3 matrix has three.
