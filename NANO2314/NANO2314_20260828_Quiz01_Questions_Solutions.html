Question 1: Multiple Choice [4 pts]

Circle the letter of the correct option. Each part is worth 1 point. Correct answers are highlighted in maroon with a check.

(a)
A derivative is often described as “rise over run” taken to the limit of an infinitesimally small run. Which two fundamental mathematical operations survive that limit?
  1. Multiplication and summation
  2. Difference and division
  3. Addition and multiplication
  4. Summation and division
Solution

(B) Difference and division. HW 8/24 P1: rise and run are differences; “over” is division.

(b)
A nanoscale motor's speed is $$v(t) = \begin{cases} 2t & \text{for } 0 \le t \le 5,\\ 10 & \text{for } t > 5, \end{cases}$$ in $\mu$m/s, with $t$ in seconds. The motor's acceleration at $t = 6$ s is:
  1. $0\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$
  2. $2\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$
  3. $10\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$
  4. $20\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$
Solution

(A) $0\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$. HW 8/24 P2: slope of a constant ($v = 10$) is zero.

(c)
Using the power rule, $\displaystyle \frac{d}{dt}\left(6 t^4\right) =$
  1. $\dfrac{6}{5} t^5$
  2. $6 t^3$
  3. $24 t^3$
  4. $24 t^5$
Solution

(C) $24\, t^3$. HW 8/26 P3: power rule, $6 \cdot 4\, t^{4-1}$.

(d)
A rally car averages $120$ km/h while actively driving a circuit. Using $1.6\ \text{km} = 1$ mile, this average speed in miles per hour is approximately:
  1. $190$ mph
  2. $75$ mph
  3. $120$ mph
  4. $37$ mph
Solution

(B) $75$ mph. HW 8/24 P5: $120 \div 1.6 = 75$.

Question 2: Free Response [6 pts]

A nanoscale sensor pod accelerates from rest with speed $$v(t) = \begin{cases} 3t & \text{for } 0 \le t \le 4,\\ 12 & \text{for } t > 4, \end{cases}$$ in $\mu$m/s, with $t$ in seconds. Answer all parts below. Show your work — partial credit will be awarded.

(a) [1 pt] Sketch $v(t)$ for $0 \le t \le 6$ s.
Solution

Reference sketch of $v(t)$: straight line from $(0,0)$ to $(4,12)$, then a horizontal line at $v = 12$ through $t = 6$ s.

Slope of the rising segment is $3\ \mu\text{m/s}^2$; for $t > 4$ the slope (acceleration) is $0$.
(b) [2 pts] Write down the integral for the displacement $x(t) = \displaystyle\int_0^{t} v(\tau)\, d\tau$ for $t \le 4$ s. Evaluate that integral.

Hint: $\tau$ is a “dummy variable” standing in for time inside the integral, so the answer comes out as a function of the upper limit $t$, not of $\tau$.

Solution

With $\tau$ as the dummy time variable, the displacement is

$$x(t) = \int_0^{t} v(\tau)\, d\tau = \int_0^{t} 3\tau\, d\tau = \frac{3}{2}t^2 \qquad (t \le 4).$$

So $x(t) = $\tfrac{3}{2}t^2$$ for $t \le 4$ s ($\tau$ is the dummy variable; $t$ is the upper limit).

(c) [2 pts] Find the displacement at $t = 6$ s. (In other words, how much has the pod moved?) Include units in your final answer.
Solution

From part (b), $x(4) = 24\ \mu$m. For $t > 4$, the pod moves at constant speed $12\ \mu$m/s:

$$x(t) = x(4) + 12\,(t - 4) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x(6) = 24 + 12(2) = \boxed{\,48\ \mu\text{m}\,}.$$
(d) [1 pt] Sketch $x(t)$ for $0 \le t \le 6$ s.
Solution

Reference sketch of $x(t)$: upward-opening parabola from $(0,0)$ through $(4, 24)$, transitioning smoothly (slope $= 12$) into a straight line through $(6, 48)$.

Parabola $x = \tfrac{3}{2}t^2$ for $t \le 4$ (slope at $t=4$ is $12$), continuing as a straight line of slope $12$ for $t > 4$.
