NANO 2314 Homework: Two Frequencies in One Cantilever

10 points, plus up to 5 points of extra credit. Assigned with the Fourier Transform (Day 3) lecture, October 19, 2026.

Background

An atomic force microscope (AFM) images a surface with a sharp tip on a tiny vibrating cantilever. A cantilever has several vibration modes; for a rectangular beam the second mode is at f₂ ≈ 6.27 f₁. In bimodal AFM, both modes are driven at once: mode 1 tracks topography, while mode 2 senses stiffness and stickiness. The data are 500 µs of such a cantilever’s steady, noisy oscillation.

Data: 261021-bimodal_AFM-data.csv, download at https://tinyurl.com/N2314-bimodal-AFM-data (time_us in µs, deflection_nm in nm; 5000 points, every 0.1 µs).

Problems

1. By eye (5 points)

  1. Plot deflection versus time for the first 50 µs, using Excel, Google Colab, or another plotting tool. Attach or upload your graph.
  2. Estimate the periods T₁ (large, slow oscillation) and T₂ (small, fast ripple). Tip: time several cycles and divide by the number of cycles.
  3. Calculate f₁ = 1/T₁ and f₂ = 1/T₂ in kHz. Is f₂/f₁ close to 6.27, the value for a rectangular cantilever?

2. By Fourier transform (5 points)

  1. Use Google Colab or another AI-assisted tool to Fourier-transform all 500 µs of data (using all of it gives the best resolution). Plot the spectrum from 0 to 1000 kHz, and read f₁ and f₂ from the two peaks. Attach or upload your graph.
  2. Zoom in on one peak. What is the spacing between neighboring frequency points (the frequency resolution)?
  3. Compare your f₁ and f₂ from Problems 1 and 2. Which method is more precise?

3. Extra credit, up to 5 points: By curve fitting

  1. Using Google Colab or another AI-assisted tool, curve-fit the first 50 µs of data. What is the fit function that fits the data well? Attach or upload your graph. (Hint 1: include a phase for each frequency. Hint 2: use your by-eye f₁ and f₂ as initial guesses. Hint 3: also give initial guesses for the amplitudes A₁ and A₂.)
  2. How do your fitted f₁ and f₂ compare with Problems 1 and 2? How precise are they (give the uncertainties)?
  3. What information can you get from curve fitting that you cannot get from the Fourier transform?
