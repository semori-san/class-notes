  1. 1. (6 pts) As in the Fourier transform homework, a signal is multiplied by a reference. The signal is a 20 kHz sine wave and the reference is a 5 kHz sine wave; both have the same amplitude.

    1. (2 pts) Find the period of the signal and the period of the reference. Don’t forget the units.

      Solution — part (a) of question 1

      T=1f,1kHz=103Hz=103s−1T = \frac{1}{f}, \qquad 1\ \text{kHz} = 10^{3}\ \text{Hz} = 10^{3}\ \text{s}^{-1}

      ↓ the signal: write kilo as a power of ten

      Tsig=120×103s−1=120×10−3s=0.05×10−3s=5×10−5s=50μsT_{\text{sig}} = \frac{1}{20 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s}^{-1}} = \frac{1}{20} \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 0.05 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 5 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{s} = 50\ \mu\text{s}

      ↓ the reference, 5 kHz: the same steps

      Tref=15×103s−1=15×10−3s=0.2×10−3s=2×10−4s=200μsT_{\text{ref}} = \frac{1}{5 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s}^{-1}} = \frac{1}{5} \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 0.2 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 2 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{s} = 200\ \mu\text{s}

      Why: 10310^{3} in the denominator becomes 10−310^{-3} on top; 10−310^{-3} s is a millisecond and 10−610^{-6} s a microsecond, so 0.05×10−30.05 \times 10^{-3} s =0.05= 0.05 ms =50μ= 50\ \mus.

      Conclusion

      Tsig=50μsT_{\text{sig}} = 50\ \mu\text{s} and Tref=200μsT_{\text{ref}} = 200\ \mu\text{s}: one reference period holds 4 signal periods.

      In words. The period is one over the frequency. Write the kilo as 10310^{3}; one over it is 10−310^{-3}, a milli. So the 20 kHz signal repeats every 120×10−3\tfrac{1}{20} \times 10^{-3} s = 0.05 ms = 50 μs, and the 5 kHz reference every 15×10−3\tfrac{1}{5} \times 10^{-3} s = 0.2 ms = 200 μs, four times as long.

    3. (1 pt) What is the integral of the product from t=0t = 0 to the largest time shown in (b)? How do you know? From this, conclude whether the signal and the reference are orthogonal.

      Solution — part (c) of question 1

      the integral is the area under the product curve

      ↓ every positive hump has a matching negative one

      first half: ++small, −-big, ++big, −-small; second half: the same with the signs flipped

      ↓ the areas cancel

      Conclusion

      ∫0200μs(signal)(reference)dt=0⇒orthogonal\int_{0}^{200\ \mu\text{s}} (\text{signal})(\text{reference})\,dt = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{orthogonal}

      In words. The positive and negative humps of the product are equal in size, so the area, the integral, is zero. A zero integral of the product is what orthogonal means: the 5 kHz reference finds no 5 kHz in the 20 kHz signal.

Write a system of two equations that gives ana_{n} and wnw_{n} in terms of the previous year’s populations, an−1a_{n-1} and wn−1w_{n-1}.

    Solution for question 2

    0.95an−10.95\,a_{n-1}

    ↓

    stay in Arendelle (1−0.051 - 0.05)

    +plus

    0.15wn−10.15\,w_{n-1}

    ↓

    ↓ add them; then the same for Weaselton

    an=0.95an−1+0.15wn−1a_{n} = 0.95\,a_{n-1} + 0.15\,w_{n-1}

    wn=0.05an−1+0.85wn−1w_{n} = 0.05\,a_{n-1} + 0.85\,w_{n-1}

Where the class had the most trouble

These cost the most points. If you lost points here, start your review with them.

  1. Problem 1(b) — the product. Only 6 of 12 products had the right signs (+, −, +, − then −, +, −, +), and only 4 had humps that grow and shrink with the reference. Some products stayed mostly above the axis, followed only the reference’s sign, or were irregular wiggles. Multiply the two curves point by point, half a signal period at a time: same signs give a positive hump, opposite signs a negative one, and the hump is small where the reference is small.
  2. Problem 1(c) — the integral and the word “orthogonal”. Half the class wrote that the integral is 0 and gave the reason: the positive and negative humps cancel. Some wrote only the integral sign with its limits, or “not orthogonal”. Orthogonal means the integral of the product is zero, so the conclusion follows from the canceling areas.
  3. Problem 1(b) — four signal cycles, one reference cycle, and the time axis. Two booklets drew the signal slower than the reference; the 20 kHz signal is the faster one. Label the time axis from the period: 50 μs per signal cycle, so 0, 50, 100, 150, 200 μs. Half the axes had no numbers, no unit, or numbers that did not match the periods from (a).
  4. Problem 1(a) — the prefix. Four booklets had the reciprocal right but the prefix or the factor of 10 wrong (0.5 ms, 0.2 ms, 5 μs) or swapped the two periods. One over kilohertz is milliseconds: 1/(20kHz)=0.05ms=50μs1/(20\ \text{kHz}) = 0.05\ \text{ms} = 50\ \mu\text{s}.
  5. Problem 3 — the staying term and the *. Dollar signs were the strong point (10 of 12). The misses: -$A$2C5 instead of (1-$A$2)C5 (the people who stay are one minus the rate), the rate multiplying the wrong cell, a number typed in place of a rate cell, and products written without *, such as ($A$1)(B5), which a spreadsheet does not accept.
