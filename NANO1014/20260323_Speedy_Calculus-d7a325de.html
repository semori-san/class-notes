Why Are We Doing This?

Today, we are going to set aside the cooling drinks and the laptops for a minute and do a really fast calculus review. Why? Because in the real world, data doesn’t come in neat, tidy mathematical equations—it comes in messy, jagged lines. We need to understand the physical intuition behind calculus so we can look at a chaotic graph of a rally car tearing down a dirt road at 100 miles per hour and figure out exactly how many G-forces the driver is feeling and how far they’ve traveled.

TL;DR — Key Takeaways

Calculus boils down to two main visual concepts: taking a derivative means finding the slope of a curve, and taking an integral means finding the area under a curve. We applied these concepts to real-world speedometer data from a rally car. By finding the slope of the speed graph, we calculated the car’s acceleration (which maxed out around 1 G), and by estimating the area under the speed graph, we figured out the total displacement (about 7 kilometers).

Learning Objectives

By the end of these notes, you should be able to:

  • Explain the graphical meaning of a derivative (slope) and an integral (area).

  • Differentiate and integrate simple polynomial functions using visual intuition.

  • Convert speed units from kilometers per hour (km/h) to meters per second (m/s).

  • Estimate the total displacement from a messy, real-world speed-versus-time graph.

Before You Start

You should be comfortable reading basic graphs (x and y axes) and know the fundamental definitions of kinematics: speed is the rate of change of distance, and acceleration is the rate of change of speed. You won’t need to memorize complex calculus formulas, but you should remember how to calculate the area of a rectangle and a triangle.
