Exactly as administered on Friday, September 4, 2026 — 10 questions worth 1 point each, plus one extra-credit question.

  1. Evaluate (10−5)3\left(10^{-5}\right)^{3}.

    1. 101510^{15}
    2. 10−210^{-2}
    3. 3×10−53\times10^{-5}
    4. 10−810^{-8}
    5. 10−1510^{-15}
    Solution for question 1

    Correct answer: E. 10−1510^{-15}

    Why: A power raised to a power multiplies the exponents: (−5)×3=−15(-5)\times 3 = -15, so the answer is 10−1510^{-15}. The distractor 10−210^{-2} comes from adding 33 instead of multiplying, and 3×10−53\times10^{-5} comes from multiplying the whole quantity by 33 rather than raising it to the third power.

  2. Which list is ordered correctly from smallest to largest?

    1. Atom →\rightarrow glucose molecule →\rightarrow COVID virus →\rightarrow red blood cell
    2. Glucose molecule →\rightarrow atom →\rightarrow COVID virus →\rightarrow red blood cell
    3. Atom →\rightarrow COVID virus →\rightarrow glucose molecule →\rightarrow red blood cell
    4. Atom →\rightarrow glucose molecule →\rightarrow red blood cell →\rightarrow COVID virus
    5. Red blood cell →\rightarrow COVID virus →\rightarrow glucose molecule →\rightarrow atom
    Solution for question 2

    Correct answer: A. Atom →\rightarrow glucose molecule →\rightarrow COVID virus →\rightarrow red blood cell

    Why: Climb the ladder one decade at a time: an atom at ∼10−10\sim10^{-10}\,m, a glucose molecule at ∼10−9\sim10^{-9}\,m, a COVID virus at ∼10−7\sim10^{-7}\,m, a red blood cell at ∼10−5\sim10^{-5}\,m. Every step here is at least one order of magnitude, so nothing is a close call. One of the wrong choices is the correct list read backwards — the question asks for smallest to largest, so check the direction before you check the order.

  3. A silicon wafer is 300μ300\ \mum thick. Expressed in millimeters, that thickness is:

    1. 0.030.03 mm
    2. 0.30.3 mm
    3. 33 mm
    4. 3030 mm
    5. 300300 mm
    Solution for question 3

    Correct answer: B. 0.30.3 mm

    Why: Anchor to meters first: 300μm=300×10−6m=3×10−4m300\ \mu\text{m} = 300\times10^{-6}\,\text{m} = 3\times10^{-4}\,\text{m}. Since 1mm=10−31\,\text{mm}=10^{-3}\,m, 3×10−4m=3×10−1mm=0.33\times10^{-4}\,\text{m} = 3\times10^{-1}\,\text{mm} = 0.3\,mm. Sanity check: a millimeter is a fat sharpie line, and a wafer is visibly thinner than that, so a number below 11\,mm is right. The 300300\,mm choice is the trap of copying the number across without converting it at all.

  4. Which of the following is about 10−910^{-9} m across?

    1. A COVID virus
    2. An E. coli bacterium
    3. A glucose molecule (a simple sugar)
    4. The thickness of a lipid bilayer (cell membrane)
    5. A single atom
    Solution for question 4

    Correct answer: C. A glucose molecule (a simple sugar)

    Why: 10−910^{-9}\,m is 11\,nm, the width of a simple sugar molecule. Moving up the ladder: a lipid bilayer is one decade larger at 10−810^{-8}\,m and a COVID virus two decades larger at 10−710^{-7}\,m. Moving down, a single atom is one decade smaller at 10−1010^{-10}\,m.

  5. A square display panel of area 10−210^{-2} m2^{2} is completely covered by 10610^{6} identical square pixels. How wide is one pixel?

    1. 10−810^{-8} m2^{2}
    2. 10−410^{-4} m
    3. 10−410^{-4} m2^{2}
    4. 10−210^{-2} m
    5. 10−210^{-2} m2^{2}
    Solution for question 5

    Correct answer: B. 10−410^{-4} m

    Why: Area of one pixel: 10−2m2106=10−8m2\frac{10^{-2}\,\text{m}^{2}}{10^{6}}=10^{-8}\,\text{m}^{2}. The pixel is a square, so w=10−8m2=10−8/2m=10−4w=\sqrt{10^{-8}\,\text{m}^{2}}=10^{-8/2}\,\text{m}=10^{-4}\,m, about a tenth of a millimeter — right for a real screen. 10−8m210^{-8}\,\text{m}^{2} is the area of one pixel, step one with the square root skipped; 10−4m210^{-4}\,\text{m}^{2} reports a width in area units; and 10−210^{-2}\,m and 10−2m210^{-2}\,\text{m}^{2} belong to the whole panel.

  6. The area of a human fingernail plate is approximately equal to:

    1. 10210^{2} mm3^{3}
    2. 10210^{2} mm2^{2}
    3. 10210^{2} mm
    4. 1010 mm2^{2}
    5. 1010 mm
    Solution for question 6

    Correct answer: B. 10210^{2} mm2^{2}

    Why: A fingernail plate is about 11\,cm across, so its area is of order 1cm21\,\text{cm}^{2}. Start from the 1D fact 1cm=101\,\text{cm}=10\,mm and square both sides: (1cm)2=(10mm)2=102mm2=100mm2(1\,\text{cm})^{2}=(10\,\text{mm})^{2}=10^{2}\,\text{mm}^{2}=100\,\text{mm}^{2}. Squaring hits the number and the unit. 1010\,mm is the 1D conversion left untouched and 10mm210\,\text{mm}^{2} squares the unit but not the number; 10210^{2}\,mm and 102mm310^{2}\,\text{mm}^{3} have the right number on the wrong kind of unit. Notice that three choices read 10210^{2} and two read 1010, so the number alone will not get you there — an area must end in mm2\text{mm}^{2}.

  7. A computer chip has an area of 10−410^{-4} m2^{2}. Each transistor occupies an area of about 10−1410^{-14} m2^{2}. Roughly how many transistors fit on the chip?

    1. 10−510^{-5}
    2. 10510^{5}
    3. 10510^{5} m2^{2}
    4. 101010^{10}
    5. 101010^{10} m2^{2}
    Solution for question 7

    Correct answer: D. 101010^{10}

    Why: How-many-fit is always big area divided by small area: 10−4m210−14m2=(10−410−14)×(m2m2)=10−4−(−14)×1=1010\frac{10^{-4}\,\text{m}^{2}}{10^{-14}\,\text{m}^{2}} =\left(\frac{10^{-4}}{10^{-14}}\right)\times\left(\frac{\text{m}^{2}}{\text{m}^{2}}\right) = 10^{-4-(-14)}\times 1 = 10^{10}. The m2\text{m}^{2} cancels top against bottom, so the answer is a count — a pure number with no unit. Any choice still carrying m2\text{m}^{2} is wrong no matter what the number says. 10−510^{-5} is the one-dimensional ratio turned upside down — the fraction of one chip edge taken up by a single transistor’s edge — and 10510^{5} is that ratio itself, how many transistors fit along a single edge. (Ten billion transistors on a 1cm21\,\text{cm}^{2} chip is about right for a modern processor.)

  8. A sesame seed is about 10−310^{-3} m wide and a red blood cell is about 10−510^{-5} m across. How many orders of magnitude larger is the area of the sesame seed?

    1. 10410^{4}
    2. 10210^{2}
    3. 66
    4. 44
    5. 22
    Solution for question 8

    Correct answer: D. 44

    Why: One order of magnitude is one factor of ten. In 1D the seed is 10−3/10−5=10210^{-3}/10^{-5}=10^{2} times wider, which is 2 orders of magnitude. Area goes as width squared, so the areas differ by (102)2=104\left(10^{2}\right)^{2}=10^{4} — 4 orders of magnitude. 22 is the 1D answer and 66 is the 3D one. 10210^{2} and 10410^{4} are the ratios themselves: the question asks how many factors of ten, so the answer is the exponent, not ten raised to it.

  9. 11 m3^{3} is equal to:

    1. 10310^{3} mm3^{3}
    2. 10610^{6} mm
    3. 10610^{6} mm3^{3}
    4. 10910^{9} mm2^{2}
    5. 10910^{9} mm3^{3}
    Solution for question 9

    Correct answer: E. 10910^{9} mm3^{3}

    Why: Start from 1m=1031\,\text{m}=10^{3}\,mm and cube both sides: 1m3=(103)3mm3=109mm31\,\text{m}^{3}=\left(10^{3}\right)^{3}\text{mm}^{3}=10^{9}\,\text{mm}^{3}. You get a factor of 10001000 from each of the three directions. 103mm310^{3}\,\text{mm}^{3} and 106mm310^{6}\,\text{mm}^{3} stop at the 1D and 2D conversions, 10610^{6}\,mm puts that 2D number on a length, and 109mm210^{9}\,\text{mm}^{2} has the right number on an area.

  10. In this course, what is a transistor?

    1. A tiny transforming robot that changes shape to do mechanical work inside the chip
    2. An electrically controlled switch: a voltage on one terminal turns the current flowing between the other two terminals on or off
    3. A metal wire that carries current between the layers of a chip without controlling it
    4. A material whose electrical resistance falls to exactly zero when it is cooled below a critical temperature
    5. A tiny lens that focuses ultraviolet light onto the silicon surface during chip manufacturing
    Solution for question 10

    Correct answer: B. An electrically controlled switch: a voltage on one terminal turns the current flowing between the other two terminals on or off

    Why: A transistor is a switch with no moving parts. A voltage on the gate decides whether current can flow from source to drain, so the device holds a 11 or a 00. That is why packing more of them onto a chip means more computing. Of the other choices, three describe real things you would find in a chip fab — a superconductor, a photolithography optic, and an interconnect wire — but none of them is a transistor. The shape-shifting robot is not a real thing at all: a transistor has no moving parts, which is exactly why it can switch billions of times a second and never wear out.

  11. Extra credit. MOSFET stands for:

    1. Multi-Output Semiconductor Frequency-Enhanced Transistor
    2. Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Emission Transistor
    3. Metallic Overlayer Silicon Ferroelectric Transistor
    4. Molecular–Oxide–Silicon Fully-Etched Transistor
    5. Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor
    Solution for question 11

    Correct answer: E. Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor

    Why: Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor. The name is a recipe for the device: a metal gate sitting on a thin insulating oxide layer, which sits on the semiconductor (silicon). The electric field from the gate — not any current into it — is what switches the channel underneath on and off.

  9. The classroom floor measures 1010 m ×\times 1010 m and a sesame seed measures 11 mm ×\times 11 mm. How many orders of magnitude larger is the area of the floor?

    Correct answer: D. 88

    Why: Along one edge the floor is 101m/10−3m=10410^{1}\,\text{m}/10^{-3}\,\text{m}=10^{4} times longer, which is 4 orders of magnitude.   11. Extra credit. CMOS stands for:

    1. Controlled Metal–Oxide Sensor
    2. Crystalline Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor
    3. Complementary Micro-Optimal Switch
    4. Compact Molecular–Oxide–Silicon

    Solution for question 11

    Correct answer: E. Complementary Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor

  8. A computer chip is about 10−210^{-2} m on a side and a transistor is about 10−710^{-7} m on a side. How many orders of magnitude larger is the area of the chip?

  10. Think of a transistor as a water faucet, with electric current in place of water. What plays the role of the handle you turn to start or stop the flow?

    1. The temperature of the silicon the transistor is built on
    2. The metal wire that carries current away to the next transistor
    3. The voltage applied to the gate terminal
    4. The current flowing from the source to the drain
    5. The thin oxide layer sitting underneath the gate
    Solution for question 10

    Correct answer: C. The voltage applied to the gate terminal

    Why: Turning the handle is applying a voltage to the gate. That voltage is the input that decides whether a conducting channel forms underneath it, and so whether current may flow from source to drain. In the same picture the source-to-drain current is the water — the thing being controlled, not the thing doing the controlling, and mistaking one for the other is the main trap here. The interconnect wire is the pipe carrying the water onward, and the oxide is the washer that seals the handle to the valve: it lets the gate act through an electric field while blocking any current from leaking into it.

  11. Extra credit. In a doped semiconductor, what is the difference between n-type and p-type?

    1. n-type forms the wiring on a chip, while p-type forms the oxide
    2. n-type conducts electricity, while p-type is an insulator
    3. n-type has nitrogen and p-type has phosphorus
    4. n-type is made of silicon, while p-type is made of germanium
    5. n-type carries current with extra electrons (negative); p-type carries it with holes (positive)
    Solution for question 11

    Correct answer: E. n-type carries current with extra electrons (negative); p-type carries it with holes (positive)

    Why: The letters refer to the sign of the mobile charge carriers, not to the charge of the material. Doping silicon with an element that brings spare electrons gives n-type, where the current is carried by those extra electrons (n for negative). Doping with an element that is one electron short leaves holes behind, and a hole moves like a positive carrier, giving p-type (p for positive). The first distractor is the trap: it matches the letters — nitrogen for n-type, phosphorus for p-type — but the chemistry is backwards. Phosphorus, with five valence electrons, is the classic n-type dopant (one spare electron), and boron, with three, is the classic p-type dopant (one hole); nitrogen is not a standard silicon dopant at all. Putting n-type and p-type silicon next to each other builds the junctions that a MOSFET is made of.
